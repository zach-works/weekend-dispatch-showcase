Bird of the Week
vii.
White-throated Sparrow
Zonotrichia albicollis — The winter sparrow scratching through leaf litter under every park shrub
Where
On the ground under shrubs and along path edges in any park, often in loose flocks.
ID
A crisp white throat patch, a yellow spot between the eye and bill, and a striped crown. The stripes come in two forms, white-striped and tan-striped.
Voice
A thin whistled song, often written “Oh sweet Canada, Canada, Canada.”
Range
Breeds in Canada’s boreal forest; winters across the eastern U.S., including NYC.
Conservation angle

Still common, but a frequent victim of window collisions during migration. It is also a genetics case study: the two stripe forms almost always pair with the opposite form.

Tree of the Week
viii.
Bigleaf Maple
Acer macrophyllum — The maple with the biggest leaves in the world, and a garden of moss on every limb
Where
Moist valley bottoms and ravines across coastal B.C. and the Pacific Northwest.
Leaf
Deeply five-lobed and often wider than a dinner plate.
Epiphytes
Trunks and limbs are carpeted in moss and licorice fern, a canopy garden that can outweigh the tree’s own leaves.
Seeds
Paired, hairy samaras held at a sharp angle.
Conservation angle

It enriches forest soils and streams; its leaves break down quickly and feed the food webs of salmon streams.

Then & Now
x.
Gowanus was a creek before it was a canal
Then

Gowanus Creek was a tidal inlet winding through salt marsh, known for its oysters. In the Battle of Brooklyn in 1776, the Maryland troops’ stand near the Old Stone House held off the British long enough for American soldiers to retreat across the creek’s marshes.

Now

The creek was dredged and bulkheaded into the Gowanus Canal in the mid-1800s and ringed with industry. It was designated an EPA Superfund site in 2010. Cleanup means dredging contaminated sediment and building tanks to capture combined sewer overflows.

xi.
Pacific Coast · Forest & Sea
Southern Resident Killer Whales
Orcinus orca — the J, K and L pods of the Salish Sea
Who
Three family groups that spend much of the year in the waters between Vancouver Island, Vancouver and Seattle.
Diet
Specialists in salmon, above all Chinook, the largest salmon species.
Culture
Each pod has its own dialect of calls, passed down through the generations.
Conservation angle

Listed as endangered in both Canada and the U.S., with fewer than 80 whales. Their three main threats are too little Chinook salmon, pollutants in the food web, and noise and disturbance from boats.
